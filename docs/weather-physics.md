# Local weather physics and severe weather

The standalone Mushoku Tensei: Weather mod simulates climate and hazards only in loaded areas near players. Broad regional fronts use its biome-, temperature-, pressure-, and wind-aware climate model; they do not enable dimension-wide vanilla rain. Particles are emitted around players rather than across the whole dimension. When Mushoku Tensei: Magic is also installed, Cumulonimbus creates a server-authoritative sector whose rain is rendered through a client-local vanilla weather override. Weather does not depend on Magic; Magic requires Weather.

## Regional weather and moving hazards

- Exposed living entities accumulate temporary wetness in rain and dry over time; snow and hail wet them more slowly. Heavy precipitation extinguishes exposed flames and can put out uncovered fire blocks.
- Strong regional wind nudges exposed creatures. Tornado cores add a rotating, inward-flowing wind field and a vertical updraft that can lift nearby creatures.
- Warm, humid rotating thunderstorms can form supercells; strong gust fronts can form fast-moving squall lines; low-pressure tropical storms can form cyclones; and thunderstorms can also produce localized tornadoes or cool-core hail. Hot, dry biomes can form drifting sandstorms. Systems pass through smooth forming, mature, optional strengthening, and dissipating phases, then expire. Their weather fields and particles are local and do not load chunks.
- Storm and regional clouds are GPU-rendered volumetric density fields, ray-marched through bounded 3D boxes in a GLSL fragment shader. Procedural erosion noise and per-kind density functions form cumulonimbus towers and anvils, rotating supercell wall clouds, wind-perpendicular squall shelves, spiral cyclone bands, ground-reaching tornado funnels, dust walls, and stratiform overcast. Render quality controls both ray steps and the number of active volumes; quality 3 is the high-detail default, while 1 and 2 trade detail for performance. Distance fades reduce hard popping. Particles are restricted to secondary effects such as rain, hail, wind streaks, lightning, and ground-level dust—never the cloud bodies themselves. Tornadic dust gets bounded tangential, inward, and upward motion without expensive all-pairs particle collisions.
- `config/mushoku_weather.json` belongs to the standalone Weather mod. `severeWeatherEnabled` controls natural supercells, squalls, cyclones, tornadoes, hailstorms, and sandstorms. Spawn chances, system size/strength, mature-stage strengthening, and the active-system cap are configurable. `weatherBlockDamage` defaults to `false`; when enabled, tornadoes and cyclones may remove blocks up to `maxBlocksPerStorm` (default 64, hard cap 512) and only below `maxDamageableBlockHardness` (default 2.0, hard cap 3.0). Visual and entity effects continue with block damage disabled. Existing weather toggles migrate from `mushoku_magic.json` the first time the new config is created.
- Cumulonimbus is a player-cast storm limited to exactly 20×20 chunks and requires the dependent Magic mod. Its active sector and lifetime are synchronized to modded clients; a client inside the sector locally blends vanilla rain and thunder gradients, then restores its saved values on exit. The server and other dimensions' weather are untouched. WeatherPhysics uses the same sector state for wetness and fire suppression; the Magic integration adds the spell modifiers. Vanilla block precipitation ticks are not simulated. Set `regionalWeatherEnabled` to `false` to use vanilla weather for the regional system instead.
- While regional weather is enabled, the Weather manager intentionally holds vanilla's global weather clear. Operators can use `/magicweather <clear|cloudy|rain|thunder|snow> [seconds]` for an explicit local override (default 120 seconds, 160-block radius), or `/magicweather status` to inspect sampled cloud cover, precipitation, wind, enabled cloud rendering, and nearby severe systems. `/magicweather hail`, `squall`, `supercell`, `tornado`, `cyclone`, and `sandstorm` start moving local hazards; these require `severeWeatherEnabled=true`. `clear` also removes nearby severe systems. `/magicweather reload` reloads the Weather config. Vanilla `/weather` controls the whole dimension only after regional weather is disabled.
- If local rain particles appear without volumetric clouds, check `volumetricCloudsEnabled=true` in the client `config/mushoku_weather.json`. The client log now reports whether a weather snapshot arrived, whether the Forge/Fabric world-render hook ran, and whether the GPU renderer actually drew a cloud volume; shader or GPU failures are logged explicitly.
- The simulation does not wet blocks, fill cauldrons, or apply crop-weather ticks.

## Spell interactions

- Wind and updraft fields influence free-flight Fire Bolt, Explosive Fireball, Water Ball, Water Cannon, Ice Needle, and Stone Ball trajectories. Lighter spells drift more; stone resists drift. Horizontal drift is capped at four blocks and vertical lift at two, and wind cannot extend a cast beyond its scaled maximum range.
- Gust and Updraft leave a brief, radius-limited air current that can affect subsequent spell paths. These fields fade in and out and are removed when the server stops.
- Rain and accumulated wetness modestly reduce Fire Bolt damage, sharply shorten its burn duration, and suppress fire-spell ignition. Heavy rain also dampens explosive fire ignition without disabling the configured block-destruction setting.
- Humid/rainy air modestly strengthens Water Cannon hits; low temperatures favor Ice Needle. These elemental adjustments are capped and multiply, rather than replace, the existing staff and spell scaling.

The effects are deliberately bounded around loaded player areas so weather adds visible and tactical feedback without overwhelming the mod's ×2, ×15, and ×50 staff multipliers or creating unbounded server work.
